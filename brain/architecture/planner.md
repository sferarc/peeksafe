# Planner

`src/plan.ts` answers "how many runs does this suite need" before anything is spent, including the cases where no budget works. `src/frontier.ts` asks the inverse question, what is certifiable inside a budget.

## The three numbers per case

From the module header in `src/plan.ts`:

1. **The bar.** e-BH at FDR `q` over `m` cases certifies a case on its own once its e-value clears `m / q`.
2. **The ceiling.** `evidenceCeilingLogE`, the limit of the unpaired e-value as candidate runs go to infinity. Under the bar, no candidate budget certifies the case; more baseline runs do.
3. **The paired alternative.** Re-running the baseline on the same seed makes the null an exact point mass at one half, so the paired e-value has no ceiling. It costs two runs per observation and is often still cheaper.

## Output

`makePlan(cases, baselines, config)` returns a `Plan` with one `PlanCase` per case and `totals`. Each case has a `detectability` of `UNPAIRED`, `PAIRED_ONLY`, `TOO_EXPENSIVE` or `IMPOSSIBLE`, the unpaired run count (or `Infinity`), the paired pair count, and `baselineRunsNeeded` (or `null`). `IMPOSSIBLE` uses the same rule as `gate`'s `impossible` flag. `totals` includes `decidableUnpaired` and `decidableBest`.

`DEFAULT_PLAN` carries cost and timing assumptions (`costPerRunUsd`, `msPerRun`, `screenRuns`, `pairCoupling` and more; see `PlanConfig`). `pairCoupling` should be measured with `pairPhi` on a pilot, not assumed: `makePlan`'s fourth argument takes a pilot pair table per case, and a case with one is priced at its `pairPhi`, floored at 0, with an empty table ignored ([[decisions/2026-10-05-plan-design-per-case]]).

Each `PlanCase` also carries `design`, the design to run it with: of unpaired and paired, the one that decides it within `runsPerCaseCeiling` in fewer runs, unpaired on a tie, `null` when neither does; and `pairCoupling`, the coupling its paired figures used. This is the same per-case choice `totals.bestRuns` sums. The expected bill (`typicalRuns`, `typicalScreenedRuns`) and both screening passes price each case at its own `design`, two runs an observation where it is paired, so `totals.runsPerObservation` is an average between 1 and 2 on a mixed suite. The suite-level `recommendedDesign` is separate: it names the design in `recommendation`, flips to paired when more than a quarter of the suite is `PAIRED_ONLY` or when more than half of the decidable cases have a paired `design`, and prices only the cases with no `design`. The second condition came after per-case pricing (#95): four cases at 85% on a 240-run baseline are all cheaper paired and none is `PAIRED_ONLY`, so the bill priced every case paired while `recommendation` named unpaired and never mentioned pairing (`test/budget.test.ts`, "recommends pairing when every decidable case is cheaper paired"). Setting `config.design` prices every case at that one design. The observation count of an unpaired case is the exact expected number of runs `shouldStop` takes to stop it when it did not move (#105): checked at `minTrials` and then every `batchSize` runs up to `maxTrials`, at the suite's size and `config.futilityConfidence`, with a dynamic program over the success count, since the decision depends on the candidate only through its counts (`expectedStopRuns` in `src/plan.ts`, memoised like `expectedSettlePairs`). It used to be `expectedSequentialSamples`, Wald's expected sample number for the SPRT, a different rule that ignores the suite size. `test/budget.test.ts` ("prices an unpaired case's typical bill at the runs shouldStop takes to stop it") pins 58 runs for four cases at 85% over 240 baseline runs against a simulated mean of 58.3 (Wald said 36), and 18 for 50 cases at 90% over 60 against 17.1 (Wald said 28). `expectedSequentialSamples` counts each case the same way, at its `trueRate` and a suite of `cases.length` (#115), so at the default rate it equals the unpaired `typicalRuns`; `test/budget.test.ts` ("counts expectedSequentialSamples at a candidate rate in the runs shouldStop takes") pins 75 runs for a case at 70% against an 85% baseline in a suite of four, against a simulated mean of 73.7. `evaluatePoint` (so `computeFrontier`) and `affordabilityGrid` count an unpaired typical bill with the same `expectedStopRuns` (#106), at `baselineRate` as both the baseline and the candidate rate and the point's or cell's case count and `mde`; `typicalObservationsPerCase` is still Wald's figure and nothing bills from it. `test/budget.test.ts` ("prices an unpaired typical bill at the runs shouldStop takes, as makePlan does") pins the frontier against `makePlan` at 58, 18 and 49 runs (Wald said 36, 28 and 36), 82 at a `futilityConfidence` of 0.99, and an unpaired grid cell of 20 cases at 85% over 240 runs at 188 typical runs (Wald's figure gave 152). The frontier's default headline moved with it: nothing fits $100 on the typical bill and the cheapest certifiable point is 10 cases at 35 points, $163 ("refuses an unknown design or screen and a screen recall outside [0, 1]"). A paired case is counted in the exact expected number of pairs `shouldStopPaired` takes to stop it when it did not move (#111): the same dynamic program as `expectedStopRuns`, over the joint worse and better counts, since the paired decision depends on nothing else at a given pair count, at the case's `pairCoupling`, the suite's size, `config.fdr` and `config.futilityConfidence` (`expectedSettlePairs` in `src/plan.ts`). States below 1e-18 are dropped and results are memoised, because the frontier prices the same paired cell once per baseline and screen. Before that it was the first pair count at which the settle e-value on the mean trajectory ruled out a drop of `mde` (#98), which ignored the variance: at coupling 0 on 85% the mean trajectory never settled before the cap, so the plan said 96 pairs where the simulated mean is 65.6. `test/budget.test.ts` ("prices a paired case's typical bill at the pairs shouldStopPaired takes to settle it") pins 37 pairs against a simulated mean of 36.5 at 75% and coupling 0.9 (the SPRT said 40), 43 against 42.3 at 90% and 0.6 (the SPRT said 28), and 66 against 65.6 at 85% and coupling 0. The level is `config.futilityConfidence`, the stopping rule's default 0.95 when unset (#103). `test/budget.test.ts` ("prices a paired case's typical bill at the caller's futilityConfidence") pins 53 pairs at 0.99 on the 75% case against a simulated mean of 52.1, and 60 at 90% and coupling 0.6 against 60.4. `makePlan`, `evaluatePoint` and `affordabilityGrid` refuse a set level outside (0, 1). `evaluatePoint` (so `computeFrontier`) and `affordabilityGrid` count a paired typical bill with the same `expectedSettlePairs` (#100), at `baselineRate`, `pairCoupling` and the point's or cell's case count and `mde`. `test/budget.test.ts` ("prices a paired typical bill at the pairs to settle, as makePlan does") pins 35 pairs on the frontier's defaults where the SPRT said 32, 43 at 90% and coupling 0.6 where it said 28, and a paired grid cell of 50 cases at 85% over 60 runs and coupling 0.6 at 880 typical runs where it said 720. `test/budget.test.ts` ("prices the expected bill and the screening pass per case") measures 272 runs on a four-case mixed suite, and `test/readme.test.ts` pins the README's 140. Each case's share is `PlanCase.typicalRuns` (#113), and `totals.typicalRuns` is their sum. `planCase` computes it at `config.design`, else the case's `design`, else unpaired, because it cannot see the suite; `makePlan` reprices a case with no `design` when the suite recommends pairing. `test/budget.test.ts` ("reports each case's expected bill") pins the shares and the standalone figures.

## Things that have been wrong before

- `baselineRunsNeeded` bisected a quantity that is not monotone: the ceiling is a sawtooth in the baseline size because the success count is an integer. It now scans for the first crossing (#17). `test/budget.test.ts` checks it against a scan.
- The worst-case cost could come in under the expected cost (#16).
- `sprtExpectedN` returned negative run counts for a true rate between the two hypotheses, so affected cases were priced at the per-case cap (#26). The expected sample number is still Wald's approximation and under-states the SPRT's true value (`CHANGELOG.md`); `makePlan` no longer bills unpaired cases from it (#105).
- The same cap fallback again, from a different cause: `makePlan` and `evaluatePoint` validated `mde`, `fdr`, `pairCoupling` and the rest but never `alpha` or `beta`, so an error rate outside `(0, 1)` reached `sprtExpectedN`, which answers `NaN` at or above 1 and `Infinity` at 0. Both callers gate on `Number.isFinite(raw) && raw > 0` and quote `maxTrials` when that fails, and both answers fail it, so a plan priced every case at the cap with no error anywhere. Both entry points now refuse the config, and so do `sprtDecision`, `sprtExpectedN` and `sampleSizeTwoProportion`. `test/budget.test.ts` and `test/stats.test.ts` pin the refusals. Anything that reads a non-finite answer from `sprtExpectedN` as "cannot price this" needs the arguments checked before the call, because the fallback is indistinguishable from a case that honestly needs the cap.
- `sprtExpectedN` also returned `NaN` for `alpha + beta === 1`, where both walls sit at a log likelihood ratio of 0 and Wald's operating characteristic is a genuine `0/0`. It was patched to answer 0 runs, and that branch is gone now that `sprtExpectedN` refuses the sum outright (below).
- `affordabilityGrid` validated `baselineRate` and not `baselineTrials`, so a grid at zero baseline runs was priced against a Beta(1, 1) null and came back `affordable: true`, cheaper than the same grid on a real baseline. It now refuses a `baselineTrials` that is not a positive integer, as `evaluatePoint` already did. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusal and the figures.

- `planCase` is exported on its own but relied on `makePlan` for its checks, so a direct call took a -5/60 or 5/-3 baseline as `IMPOSSIBLE`, priced the paired design at a negative or `NaN` bill for a negative or `NaN` `costPerRunUsd` or `msPerRun`, and accepted `alpha + beta >= 1`. It now refuses the baseline counts with `PEEKSAFE_E_STAT_DOMAIN` and the rest with `PEEKSAFE_E_CONFIG`, through the price check `makePlan` now shares, and `makePlan` inherits the count check, so it no longer reads a -5/0 baseline as no baseline. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals.
- Error rates that sum to 1 or more were accepted. Wald's walls meet or cross there, so `sprtExpectedN` answers a small positive number (0.33 at `alpha` 0.6, `beta` 0.5) and the plan priced every case at `minTrials`. `makePlan`, `affordabilityGrid` and `evaluatePoint` now refuse `alpha + beta >= 1` through `requireErrorRates` in `src/errors.ts`. Raised in the review of #45 and recorded in #47. `sprtDecision` and `sprtExpectedN` themselves kept accepting the sum: `sprtDecision` at `alpha` 0.6, `beta` 0.5 and 0 runs answered `ACCEPT_H1`, because a log likelihood ratio of 0 already sat on the crossed H1 wall. Both now go through `requireErrorRates` too, and `test/stats.test.ts` pins the refusals with `PEEKSAFE_E_CONFIG`.
- `fixedNPerArm` called `sampleSizeTwoProportion` without the plan's `alpha` and `beta`, so the fixed-N comparator ignored them. Same source.
- `nullQuantile` reached `makePlan` through `baselineNullRate`, which refuses one outside `(0, 1)`, but `affordabilityGrid` and `typicalObservationsPerCase` (and so `evaluatePoint` and `computeFrontier`) passed it straight to `betaQuantile`. That function clamps a quantile at or below 0 to 0 and one at or above 1 to 1, so the typical bill was priced against a null rate nobody chose. All four now refuse it by name, and so does `makePlan`, before any case is read. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals. `betaQuantile` itself still clamps; a caller that takes a quantile from configuration has to check it first. It used to bisect a NaN down to about 0; it now refuses a NaN with `PEEKSAFE_E_STAT_DOMAIN`, as `normalQuantile` does (`test/stats.test.ts`).
- `fdr` was refused by `makePlan` and by nothing else in the planner. `affordabilityGrid` and `evaluatePoint` took it straight into `ebhSoloThreshold`, so an `fdr` of 2 halved the solo bar and priced a cheaper answer: 840 runs for 50 cases at 90% over a 60-run baseline, where 0.05 prices 1310, still `affordable`. An `fdr` of 0 put the bar at Infinity, which unpaired points reported as a ceiling below the bar and paired ones refused under `mcnemarSamplesForEvidence`'s name. Both now refuse it by name. `affordabilityGrid` also refuses a `costPerRunUsd` that `makePlan` would, and a case count that is not a positive integer, as `evaluatePoint` does. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals and the 1310.
- `runsPerCaseCeiling` was not checked. `makePlan` and `evaluatePoint` pass eight times it to `samplesForEvidence` and `mcnemarSamplesForEvidence` as the search cap, and at `NaN` or 0 the doubling loop never ran, so both searches tested one run, fell short and answered Infinity. Every case came back with no design, under a reason that blamed the bar or the evidence ceiling. Both entry points now refuse a ceiling that is not finite and at least 1 with `PEEKSAFE_E_CONFIG` (`requireRunCeiling` in `src/errors.ts`), and the two searches refuse a `maxN` or `maxPairs` that is not finite and at least 1 with `PEEKSAFE_E_STAT_DOMAIN`; on the cases `test/exports.test.ts` uses, at a bar of `log(4000)`, they answer 262 runs and 172 pairs. `test/budget.test.ts` and `test/exports.test.ts` pin the refusals and the figures. The bar was the other unchecked search input: at `NaN` the paired search answered 1 pair and the unpaired one Infinity, so both now refuse a `logThreshold` that is not finite with `PEEKSAFE_E_STAT_DOMAIN` (`requireLogThreshold` in `src/stats.ts`). The planners always pass a finite bar, so this reaches only direct calls.
- The batch grid (`minTrials`, `maxTrials`, `batchSize`) was checked only by `evaluatePoint`, and only for `maxTrials`. The typical bill rounds each case onto `minTrials + k * batchSize` and clamps it to `maxTrials`, so a `NaN` bound or a zero batch made `makePlan`'s `typicalRuns` `NaN`, and a `maxTrials` of 1 under a `minTrials` of 8 priced four cases at 4 unpaired runs where the defaults price 144. `requireTrialGrid` in `src/errors.ts` now refuses the grid with `PEEKSAFE_E_CONFIG` in `makePlan`, `expectedSequentialSamples`, `affordabilityGrid`, `evaluatePoint` and `typicalObservationsPerCase`. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals and the 144.
- `pairedBaselineRuns` was not checked. `evaluatePoint` prices the paired design's baseline and its typical bill from it, so a direct call at 0 runs priced the typical bill for 100 cases at 0.15 over the defaults at $2400 where 20 runs priced $6420 (before #100; the paired typical bill no longer reads it, so 20 runs now price $7220, almost all of it pairs to settle), and 2.5 runs priced a fractional baseline. `computeFrontier` refused the same value only because it passes it in as `baselineRuns`, under that name. `typicalObservationsPerCase` took its `rate`, `mde` and `baselineRuns` unchecked: at 80% over a 60-run baseline an `mde` of 0 priced 96 runs and -0.1 priced 52, and a bad rate or run count was refused under `betaQuantile`'s name. Both now refuse by name with `PEEKSAFE_E_CONFIG` or `PEEKSAFE_E_STAT_DOMAIN`. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals and the 7220.
- `evaluatePoint` did not check `budgetUsd`, `costPerRunUsd`, `costPerGradeUsd`, `screenKeepFraction` or `amortisePrs`; only `enumerateFrontier` did, before its loop. A direct call at a run cost of -1 recommended a paired point on a -$6420 bill, and a keep fraction of 2 kept 200 of 100 cases. Both now go through one `requirePricing` check in `src/frontier.ts`, which also refuses an infinite cost (against an infinite budget it recommended a point on an infinite bill). The bill lookup uses `Object.hasOwn`, as `costShares` does, so an inherited `basis` such as `"toString"` is refused rather than pricing no bill. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals and the 9200 at `amortisePrs: 1` (8400 before #100).
- `evaluatePoint` read only a `null` screen recall as unmeasured, so `NaN` (0 of 0 planted regressions caught) or a value outside [0, 1] made a screened point recommendable; on the defaults that moved the `computeFrontier` headline from 10 cases at 35 points ($83.00) to 25 points ($78.00). It now refuses a non-null recall outside [0, 1], and refuses a `design` or `screen` it does not know rather than pricing an unknown design as feasible on an infinite bill and an unknown screen as the expensive one. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals and both headlines.
- `makePlan` and `expectedSequentialSamples` did not check the case list for a repeated id, which `gate` refuses. `makePlan` priced the case twice and counted it twice in `m`, the suite size the bar `m / q` is set from, and `expectedSequentialSamples` summed it into `samples` twice while `perCase`, a map, kept it once. Both now refuse with `PEEKSAFE_E_CASE_DUPLICATE` through `requireUniqueCaseIds` in `src/errors.ts`. `test/budget.test.ts` pins the refusals.

Everything here evaluates the mean trajectory. Realised counts vary around it.
